# Landing page copy

Visitors decide on the first screen, in about five seconds. Keep a block only
if a salesperson would say it face to face. Write the copy for the blocks the
page already has; add or remove a block only when the task covers page
structure. Proof and prices come from the repository, never invented.

## Block order

First screen:

1. **Headline**: the reader's gain, or the product named plainly if it is new
   to them.
2. **Subhead**: how the headline is delivered, and who it is for.
3. **Visual**: the real product in use, showing what the headline claims.
4. **Proof**: a count, rating or recognisable names that make the headline
   believable.
5. **Action**: a button that names what the reader gets, with one line under
   it answering the main hesitation.

Below it:

6. **Features**, each written as an outcome that proves the headline. Answer
   the biggest objections here.
7. **Customer proof**: real people getting the promised result, with name,
   role and company, chosen to resemble the reader.
8. **FAQ**: the remaining objections, phrased the way customers ask them.
9. **Second action**: repeat the button and restate why to click.
10. **Founder note** (optional): name the reader's problem, own the fix, show
    what life looks like after.

## Checks

- Five seconds on the first screen: can a stranger say what it is, who it is
  for and what to do next?
- Read only headings, bold text and buttons. Is the pitch still there?
- List the top five hesitations. Is each answered on the page?
- Does every big claim have its proof on the same screen?
- Do forms ask for the minimum? Collect the rest after the reader commits.
- Are options and plans labelled by who they are for? "Basic / Pro" → "For
  one person / For a team of up to 10".
- Before the price, has the reader seen what the problem costs or what the
  dearer alternative is?
- For a demo or call, does the action say who they will meet and what it is
  not? "20 minutes with Maya. No sales pitch."
