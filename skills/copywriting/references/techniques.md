# Finding a stronger line

For a line that passes the tests but is still flat, or a blank page. Produce
candidates with several of these, then run the tests in `SKILL.md` on each.

## Ways in

- **Ladder down.** Put the vague phrase at the top. Under it answer "meaning
  what?". Repeat until an object, a number or a scene appears, and use the
  bottom rung. "Better mornings" → "less rushing" → "Breakfast ready before
  the kettle boils."
- **Opposites table.** Two columns: the old way and the new way. Fill in
  pairs and keep the sharpest. The opponent can be another approach, an
  opposing belief or a named alternative. "Easy setup" → "Other tools take an
  afternoon to set up. This takes your calendar link."
- **Borrow the familiar.** Explain a new thing through two things the reader
  already knows. "A smart planter" → "More basil than a supermarket pot, less
  care than a cactus."
- **Name what it replaces.** What does the reader stop buying or doing?
  "All-in-one project tool" → "Close four tabs."
- **Swap in a nearby object.** Trade the technical or category noun for an
  everyday thing the reader can hold. "All your tickets in the app" → "Every
  ticket in your back pocket."
- **Odd pair.** Join two things that should not fit: luxury with the mundane,
  tiny with permanent, a product that wants to be used less.
- **Reframe the number.** Pick the unit and time span that make it land, and
  set a small ask against a large total. "Saves 10 minutes a day" → "Gives
  you back a working week every year."
- **Start from one true fact.** Something observed about the product, how it
  is made or how people use it. Then find the angle. A dull fact is still a
  better seed than a slogan.
- **State the belief.** One sentence a reasonable person could disagree with:
  what the product is against.
- **Match awareness.** A reader who knows the problem gets the solution
  first. A reader who does not gets the problem first, as a question only
  they would answer yes to. "Newsletter analytics" → "Which of your emails
  made you money last month?"
- **Make the buyer feel smart.** Frame the choice as the one a sensible
  person makes.
- **Rhythm, for lines meant to be remembered.** Matched structure, opposites
  side by side, one repeated word, a dropped "and". Check: can you repeat it
  from memory after one read?
- **Story shape, for emails and about pages.** Open in one specific moment
  (time, place, what is happening). Then a person, a problem, a change. The
  customer is the one who wins.

## Working method

- Write five versions side by side, never one. Combine the best parts ("this
  clause from A inside B"), then keep the single version that passes the most
  tests.
- Judge it in the final layout: the component's real width and line breaks,
  next to its image. The image should show what the words claim.
- Check an ad does three jobs: gets noticed, says what is on offer, gives a
  reason to act now.
- It is done when no word can be added or removed to make it better.
